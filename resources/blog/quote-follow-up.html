Automated follow-up for electricians

Why Your Quotes Go Cold
(and the Follow-Up System That Fixes It).

Most electrical quotes aren't lost — they're forgotten. Here's how to build an automated follow-up system that turns estimates into signed jobs.

1. The real reason quotes go unsigned.

When a quote doesn't turn into a job, most electricians assume they were too expensive. Sometimes that's true. But far more often, the quote didn't lose — it was simply forgotten. You sent the estimate, the customer meant to think it over, life got busy on both ends, and three weeks later nobody has moved. The job didn't go to a cheaper electrician; it went to the one who followed up.

Homeowners and property managers are juggling a dozen decisions. A panel upgrade or an EV charger install is exactly the kind of "important but not urgent" project that slides down the list. Your quote isn't rejected. It's parked. And a parked quote that nobody nudges eventually goes cold for good.

2. When and how often to follow up without being pushy.

The fear of being annoying is what stops most electricians from following up at all — and it costs them jobs. The truth is that a few well-timed touches read as professional, not pushy. A simple cadence works: a same-day thank-you when you send the estimate, a friendly check-in two or three days later, another nudge at about a week, and a final "are you still looking to move forward?" around two weeks out.

Each message is short and easy to reply to. You're not badgering — you're making it easy for a busy customer to say yes. Most jobs that close on follow-up close on the second or third touch, long after the point where most shops have given up.

3. Text vs. email vs. call: what actually gets a reply.

Different customers answer on different channels, so use more than one. Text tends to get the fastest reply — a homeowner will glance at a text at a red light and fire back "yes, let's do it" in a way they never would with a phone call. Email is better for the estimate itself and for property managers who need a paper trail. A quick call still lands the bigger jobs, where the customer has questions before committing.

The winning move isn't picking one — it's sequencing them. Text for speed, email for the details, and a call to close the high-value work. Meet the customer where they actually respond.

4. How to automate follow-up so it happens whether or not you remember.

Here's the part that changes everything: none of this can depend on you remembering. You're on the tools all day — you are the least reliable follow-up system in your business. The fix is to automate it. The moment you mark an estimate as sent, a workflow starts: the thank-you text goes out, the check-ins fire on schedule, and the nudges keep coming until the customer replies or the job is won or lost.

You don't touch it. You don't have sticky notes on the dashboard or a nagging feeling that you forgot to call someone back. The system chases every quote the same way, every time, so your close rate stops depending on how good a week you're having.

5. Measuring the impact on jobs you already quoted.

The best part is that this is found money. You already did the hard work — you took the call, drove out, and priced the job. Automated follow-up just stops that effort from leaking away. Watch two numbers: how many quotes you send, and what percentage of them close. When you turn on consistent follow-up, that close rate climbs on the exact same volume of quotes.

For most shops, moving the close rate even a few points on jobs they already quoted is worth thousands a month — without a single new lead, ad dollar, or extra hour on the truck. Pick that number, watch it, and let the system do the chasing.
