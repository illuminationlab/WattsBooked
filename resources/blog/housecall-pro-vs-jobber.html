Best software for electricians

Housecall Pro vs Jobber
for Electricians (2026).

Both Housecall Pro and Jobber are solid field-service platforms for electricians; Housecall Pro leans toward marketing and consumer-facing polish, while Jobber leans toward clean workflow and ease of use. Here's how they compare — and where a purpose-built option fits.

1. The short version.

If you run an electrical business, you've almost certainly had Housecall Pro and Jobber recommended to you. They're the two names that come up most, and for good reason — both handle the core of field-service work: scheduling, dispatch, invoicing, and a customer database. Neither is a bad choice. The real question isn't which one is "better" in the abstract, but which one fits how a shop like yours actually runs the day.

Below is a fair, feature-by-feature look at the two, written for electricians rather than plumbers or landscapers. Then an honest note on a third path built specifically for electrical work.

2. Scheduling and dispatch.

This is the daily heartbeat of any electrical shop, and both platforms do it well. Jobber's calendar is widely praised for being clean and fast to learn — drag a job, assign a tech, and move on. It suits a solo electrician or a small crew who wants to get in, schedule, and get back to work without a lot of clicks.

Housecall Pro puts more emphasis on the dispatch board and real-time tech tracking, which tends to matter more once you're running several trucks at once. If you dispatch multiple electricians across a service area every day, Housecall Pro's board can feel more purpose-built for that coordination. For a one- or two-person shop, that extra machinery is often more than you need.

3. Invoicing and payments.

Both let you build a quote, convert it to an invoice, and take a card in the field. Housecall Pro is generally seen as the stronger consumer-payments experience — financing options, tip prompts, and a polished checkout that feels familiar to homeowners. That polish can nudge your close rate on bigger installs like panel upgrades or EV chargers.

Jobber's invoicing is straightforward and reliable, with clean estimates and easy follow-up on unpaid invoices. If your billing is mostly service calls and standard installs, Jobber covers it without fuss. If you lean on financing and want the slickest homeowner-facing payment flow, Housecall Pro edges ahead.

4. CRM, marketing, and ease of use.

Housecall Pro has invested heavily in marketing tools — postcards, email campaigns, review generation, and a customer database built to drive repeat business. If you want your software to actively market to past customers, it's the more feature-rich of the two out of the box.

Jobber's strength is the opposite: it stays lean and easy to use, and it's often the one electricians say they were up and running on fastest. Its client hub and follow-up features are capable without being overwhelming. As a rule of thumb, Housecall Pro suits an electrician who wants a bigger marketing engine and is willing to learn a heavier tool, while Jobber suits one who wants a clean, no-nonsense system the whole crew can pick up quickly.

5. What each costs, and who each suits.

Both are subscription products with tiered plans, and both charge more as you add users and features. Rather than quote figures that change, plans generally start in the low-to-mid hundreds per month per your latest quote — check their current pricing directly, since tiers and add-ons shift over time. The important thing is that price climbs with seats and advanced features on either platform.

So who suits which? Choose Jobber if you're a solo electrician or small crew who values speed, simplicity, and a short learning curve. Choose Housecall Pro if you run multiple trucks, care about consumer-facing payments, and want a built-in marketing engine. Both are legitimate, well-supported choices for an electrical business.

But if your biggest leak is the phone — the after-hours emergency, the call you couldn't take from a ladder — a tool built around answering and booking for electricians is worth a serious look before you sign up for a general one.
